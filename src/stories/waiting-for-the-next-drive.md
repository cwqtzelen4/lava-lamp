Hitchhiking isn't necessarily about danger or rebellion. It's about the strange mix of trust and uncertainty that comes with standing on the side of the road, waiting for a stranger to decide your next direction.

<figure class="inline-float inline-float-left inline-float-medium">
<img src="/assets/uploads/_dsf8398.jpg" alt="">
</figure>

<figure class="inline-float inline-float-right inline-float-medium">
<img src="/assets/uploads/_dsf8405.jpg" alt="">
</figure>







There is something unusual about the stillness of hitchhiking. We associate travelling with movement, yet so much of the experience is spent waiting. Drivers pass by, while you stand on the side of the road, hoping to join them. 

![](/assets/uploads/_dsf8439.jpg)

Unlike most ways of travelling, hitchhiking means giving up control over something as simple as when you'll leave. You can choose your destination, but getting there depends on the willingness of someone you've never met. There is bravery in that dependence, in raising a thumb without knowing whether anyone will stop.

And when someone finally does, trust becomes a two-way decision. One person opens the door to a stranger, while the other steps into an unfamiliar car. For a brief moment, two lives that were heading in different directions share the same road.

The journey continues, but so does the uncertainty. Another stretch of road, another wait, another stranger, and the beautiful unknown.
